The dogleg and the cluttered arena are where a purely reactive controller is weakest, and the relationship with speed is not monotonic: the slow runs exhaust the time budget negotiating obstacles while the fast runs get through. That non-monotonicity is worth showing students - it is exactly why single-point testing is not enough.

The verdict, and every discrete outcome behind it (goal reached, collision, left arena, timed out), reproduces exactly. Continuous measurements reproduce to within a fraction of a millimetre of clearance and a couple of millimetres of path length over a 90 s run through the cluttered arena: a long trajectory among obstacles is chaotic and the physics engine is not bit-exact across a whole session. `tools/run_matrix.py` enforces those tolerances, so a real regression cannot hide inside them.
